Nail Disease Detection System (NDD Project)

YOLOv8 Cropping + EfficientNetV2-CBAM Classification, served via LineBot

Snap a photo, get a preliminary nail-lesion read · 95% evaluation / 86% real-world accuracy · 1.5 s end-to-end inference · full-stack LineBot deployment on AWS

Take a photo of a nail and send it to the LineBot — the system automatically crops the nail region, classifies the lesion type, and replies with a preliminary read plus a recommended medical specialty, helping users who cannot easily visit a clinic assess risk earlier and reduce delayed care. (For reference only; not a medical diagnostic tool.)

Design Concept

  • Help users make a preliminary assessment of nail lesions.
  • Reduce delays in seeking care and improve treatment efficiency.
  • Target general users who cannot easily visit a clinic or want an early risk estimate.

Overview

The project classifies nail-lesion photos: YOLOv8 Nano automatically crops the nail region, then an EfficientNetV2 + CBAM model classifies the lesion, and the result is returned through LineBot with reference advice.

Results

  • Average evaluation accuracy 95%; real-world accuracy 86% (user smartphone photos).
  • YOLOv8 cropping averages < 200 ms; total end-to-end inference ≈ 1.5 seconds.
  • Stored records can be extended into personal diagnostic history and tracking.

Future Extensions

  • Add nearby-clinic navigation after commercialization.
  • With user consent, build personal medical records to track changes over time.
  • Extend to other appearance-based medical recognition: skin lesions, wound care, etc.

Engineering Trail

The development was not a straight line of successes but a chain of "problem → verify → fix". Key milestones:

① How to plan the overall architecture?

Solution: front/back-end separation — Nginx as a reverse proxy on AWS EC2, FastAPI + Uvicorn as the app server, YOLOv8 for image cropping and EfficientNetV2-M for classification, results written to MySQL. Extensible, async-capable, and efficient to deploy — suitable for real-time AI on limited resources (diagram in "System Architecture" above).

② Which model fits this task?

Solution: EfficientNetV2 — fewer parameters, higher accuracy, and 5–11× faster training than the alternatives.

EfficientNetV2 vs. other models: parameters / accuracy.

③ Fifteen rare lesion types with fewer than 30 photos each — now what?

Solution: during data collection, 15 of the 21 classes (including healthy nails) had < 30 samples. They were merged into a single "rare/special lesion" class, and its recommended specialty was set to dermatology — where these lesions are most commonly seen.

Sample counts of common vs. rare lesion classes.

④ The merged 7 classes were still imbalanced — the model favored the majority

Solution: Focal Loss to up-weight minority classes, preventing the majority classes from dominating the loss.

⑤ Is the model actually looking at the nail?

Solution: Score-CAM visualization of the model's attention — verifying whether decisions were based on the nail lesion rather than fingers or background.

Score-CAM: visualizing where the model looks.

⑥ Score-CAM revealed the model was NOT looking at the nail!

Solution: a manual annotation pipeline — upload the dataset to Roboflow, hand-label nail regions, train a YOLOv8 model on those labels, rebuild the entire dataset with nail-only crops, and retrain the classifier on the rebuilt images.

Roboflow manual labeling of nail regions.

⑦ Unstable recognition under unusual angles and lighting

Solution: Mixup and CutMix data augmentation to strengthen generalization and fix scenario-specific overfitting.

⑧ Above 80% accuracy, but specific classes still confused

Solution: Confusion-Matrix analysis showed frequent confusion among acral melanoma, clubbing, and nail pitting — targeted sample reinforcement for those three classes.

Confusion Matrix: locating the most-confused class pairs.

⑨ How to inspect the model's evidence and improve explainability?

Solution: SHAP masking analysis on input images, visualizing pixel-level importance to increase trust and transparency.

SHAP: pixel-level importance visualization.

⑩ Real user photos performed far worse than curated images

Solution: added a preprocessing step — every user upload is first cropped by YOLOv8 Nano to keep only the nail region, ensuring the classifier sees the right area.

⑪ Late-stage: how to push accuracy further?

Solution: integrated the CBAM attention module to sharpen feature focus on lesion regions — average evaluation accuracy reached 95%, real-world accuracy rose to 86%.
